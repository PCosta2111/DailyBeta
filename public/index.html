      How v2.0 works

      Drag the LH, RH, LF or RF handle to a hold. It snaps into place, then a popup lets you choose the grip or footwork and set the move. Drop a foot onto the upper lip to suggest a heel, underneath to suggest a toe hook, or in the middle for a toe tap. Only holds with the right shape accept hooks. Close the popup or press Escape to cancel.

      You can also click a limb, then a hold, without dragging. Keys 1/2 select hands, 3/4 select feet, D toggles Dyno and Z undoes. All controls work with Tab and Enter. Dropping away from a hold cancels the move.

      Arms, legs and torso have fixed lengths. Elbows and knees bend; the body can lean. A move that needs a longer limb causes a fall. Small footholds still perform foot switches. Match both hands on the orange top jug to send.

      For a dyno, switch Dyno ON, pick a target and pull a catching hand back like a slingshot. Both hands catch by default; the options button beside Dyno opens floating controls for LH, RH, Both, grip and keyboard force. Close it to keep the wall clear. Opening options never moves the wall. More pull means more power; release to jump. The short arrow has a fixed size and shows direction only unless Easy dyno is on. Two standing feet are needed to launch; feet cut loose after the jump.

      Switch on Easy dyno to see the hand trajectories and a suggested power percentage. Use suggested power fills in the keyboard controls so you can try a supported launch. Switch Easy dyno off to return to direction-only aiming. Your preference is remembered on this device.

      During a jump the catching arms extend toward the target. A hand can catch as it reaches the hold on the way up or down; two-hand jumps need both hands to arrive together. A little extra height is fine if you can catch on descent. Too little lift, a sideways miss, or flying past too fast still causes a fall.

      Routes mix traverses, switchbacks, compression, heel-hook setups and big dyno gaps. Holds are staggered, and an earlier handhold may become your next foothold. Every setting has a checked beta, but you'll need to read the line rather than climb two neat columns.

      After a send or fall, Show optimal beta searches this v2 wall from its original stance: fewest moves, then lowest energy. Watch on the wall steps through real v2 placements and dynos without changing your attempt or save. A beta is marked optimal only after the solver proves the bound and verifies every launch; if a launch prevents that proof, it labels the checked route as unproven instead.

      This is an animated, simplified climbing game, not a physics simulator or climbing instruction. Daily and Endless progress stay in this browser. Reduced-motion preferences skip the flight and fall animations.
